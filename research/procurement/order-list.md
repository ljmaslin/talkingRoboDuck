## What filament and its dryer mean

Filament is the 1.75 mm plastic strand on a spool: the printer melts it and
builds a part layer by layer. PLA+ is rigid plastic for shells and brackets.
TPU is flexible plastic for the designated soles and mouth pieces. These are
two different materials, not two things added to the circuit board.

A filament dryer is a small temperature-controlled heated box for the spool.
It removes absorbed moisture, especially useful for TPU, to reduce printing
problems. It sits beside the printer and never goes on the robot. It is useful
equipment rather than a prerequisite to try the first fresh PLA spool. Keep
it in the full project budget for flexible prints; follow the filament's drying
instructions. [SUNLU manufacturer description](https://www.sunlu.com/products/filadryer-s2-filament-dryer).

The £103.58 materials line consists of £17.45 PLA + £27.50 TPU + £58.63 dryer.
These are whole 1 kg spools, not an assertion that the duck consumes 2 kg. There
is no verified slicing-based material/time estimate yet. TPU 95A and PLA+ are
candidate materials; upstream has no complete validated print profile and does
not specify TPU hardness. Confirm material/fit with the matched build guide.

## Practical ordering sequence

1. Kit has been purchased. Retain the order confirmation and track dispatch.
   Board revision, UK charger and arrival estimate can be checked against the
   seller's confirmation. Seller says dispatch within 14 days, not arrival.
2. Pick one printer; printer + PLA can start the learning stage. Order the
   controller and remaining build items once happy with kit availability.
3. Check existing USB supply/cable, calipers and ties before duplicating them.
4. Print and measure test pieces before the full robot batch, then configure
   the supplied servos and assemble using the matching revision's guide.
5. Add audio after the walking robot works. No soldering station, separate PCB,
   individual motor purchases, AMS or paid printing software required here.
